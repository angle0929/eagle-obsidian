### 1. 人工智能与就业

Good morning, everyone. As a medical student who enjoys watching AI videos and even building simple knowledge bases, I’m really interested in how AI is changing the job market. Some people worry that AI will replace many jobs, especially in areas like customer service or data analysis. But personally, I take a more optimistic view. History shows that technology creates new roles even as it changes old ones. In medicine, for example, AI can assist doctors in diagnosing diseases, but it can never replace the human touch, the conversation with a patient, or the judgment needed in an emergency. I believe the key is adaptation — we need to keep learning new skills and learn how to work alongside intelligent machines. As a future anesthesiologist, I see AI as a tool that can make monitoring safer, not as something that will replace me. So I think the future of AI and employment is not about humans versus machines, but about humans and machines working together. Thank you.

### 2. 在线教育 vs 传统教育

Good morning. For me, this topic hits close to home. As a medical student, I’ve experienced both online and traditional education, especially during the pandemic. Online education is very flexible. I can replay recorded lectures as many times as I need, which is great for understanding complex subjects like pharmacology or physiology. I can also manage my study time more freely. However, medical education requires hands-on practice. You simply cannot learn to set up an IV line or perform anesthesia monitoring through a screen. Traditional classrooms give us direct interaction with professors and real practice in the lab. In my view, the best approach is a blend of both — online for theory, and traditional for clinical skills. That way, we get the convenience of digital learning without losing the human connection that is so important in medicine. Thank you.

### 3. 社交媒体与心理健康

Good morning, everyone. This is a topic I think about a lot. Social media connects us, but it can also quietly damage our mental health. From my own experience, when I scroll through apps, I often see people showing perfect lives — great achievements, perfect bodies, happy moments. It’s easy to feel anxious or that my own life isn’t good enough. Running actually helps me deal with this. When I’m on the road preparing for a half marathon, I’m away from my phone. I focus only on my breathing and my pace, and it feels so refreshing for the mind. I think many people, especially young people, compare themselves too much online and end up feeling lonely or depressed. I believe we need to set boundaries — maybe 30 minutes of social media a day, and more time in real activities like exercise, reading, or face-to-face chats. That is much healthier for our mental state. Thank you.

### 4. 环保与低碳生活

Good morning. I spend a lot of time running outdoors, and that has made me really appreciate clean air and a green environment. So the idea of low-carbon living is very important to me. We don’t have to do grand things — small changes in daily life can make a big difference. For example, I always bring my own water bottle instead of buying bottled water, and I use public transportation or walk when going short distances. I also turn off the lights when leaving a room. In Chongqing, where summers are very hot, I set the air conditioner a degree or two higher to save energy. As a student, I can also spread the word — talk to friends about reducing plastic waste, or join a campus clean-up event. Protecting the environment starts with our daily choices. If everyone lived just a little bit greener, our health and the planet’s health would both improve a lot. Thank you.

### 5. 手机依赖

Good morning. I have to be honest — I used to be quite dependent on my phone. But training for half marathons changed my relationship with it. When I run, I usually leave my phone at home. At first it felt strange, but then I discovered real freedom. Without notifications every few minutes, my mind becomes much clearer. Many of us, myself included, reach for the phone the moment we feel bored, which hurts our ability to concentrate and even messes up our sleep. As a medical student, I know that the blue light from screens can affect sleep quality, and constant distraction is bad for mental health. My advice is to make small rules: no phone during meals, one hour of offline time before bed, or leaving the phone behind during exercise. We can use technology, but we shouldn’t let it control us. A balanced life needs time without screens. Thank you.

### 6. 电子书 vs 纸质书

Good morning. I use both e-books and paper books, but for serious study, especially medical textbooks, I still prefer paper. When I read a printed book, I can highlight, write notes in the margin, and easily flip between pages. That physical feel helps me remember better. E-books are more convenient — I can carry a whole library in my tablet and search keywords quickly. However, staring at a screen for hours makes my eyes tired and dry, which isn’t great when I have long study sessions. Also, reading on a phone or tablet often leads to distraction — a message pops up and suddenly I’m on social media. So for deep learning, I choose paper; for casual reading or looking up quick information, e-books work well. Both forms will probably coexist, and it all depends on the situation and personal preference. Thank you.

### 7. 医学与健康

Good morning. As an anesthesiology student, I see health from a different perspective — not just treating disease but also keeping patients safe from hidden risks. Many people think health is about doctors and medicine, but in reality, prevention is the most powerful tool. For me personally, long-distance running is a big part of staying healthy. It strengthens my heart, clears my mind, and helps me deal with stress. I believe small, regular habits — like getting enough sleep, eating less junk food, and having regular check-ups — can prevent many serious illnesses. During my clinical visits, I’ve seen how pain and disease can take away quality of life, and that motivates me to take better care of myself and encourage others to do the same. Health is not just the absence of sickness; it’s an active state of physical and mental well-being. And it starts with the choices we make every day. Thank you.
